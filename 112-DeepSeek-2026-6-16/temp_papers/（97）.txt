Editor: Luca Fortunato Current greenhouse gas (GHG) emission research in wastewater treatment relies mainly on theoretical modeling
and lab simulations, but lacks continuous full-scale field monitoring data. Existing methods are often overly
Keywords: simplified and fail to consider the complex interactions governing emissions. Therefore, this study focused on a
Wastewater-treatment plant typical wastewater treatment plant in the eastern region and used on-site monitoring combined with multivariate
Greenhouse gas emissions

On-site monitoring

Multivariable statistics

Reduction of pollution and carbon emissions

primary sedimentation tank. Aerobic tanks dominated GHG emissions (76 % of total direct carbon emissions),
with peak N₂O flux reaching 877.9 mg/m2/d, while dissolved CH₄ concentrations peaked at 0.28 mg/L in
anaerobic zones. Further using structural equation modeling (SEM) and multiple linear regression (MLR) anal-
ysis, the correlation and impact pathways between multiple factors such as inlet and outlet water quality,
temperature, and treatment effectiveness and N2O and CH4 emissions were systematically explored. The result
revealed the direct and indirect effects of these factors on greenhouse gas emissions, achieving high accuracy
(Adj.R2 =0.91–0.94) for CH4/N2O flux. This work establishes a transferable method to advance optimization
strategies for balancing effluent quality and carbon footprint in municipal wastewater treatment plants.
