2.4.2. Direct CH4 and N2O emissions
The PPNM used to calculate direct GHG emissions was derived from
the IPCC Guidelines, in which the GHG emissions are functions of the
activity level and EFs (IPCC, 2006). The amounts of degradable organic
material and TN in the wastewater are principal factors used to deter-
mine the CH4 and N2O generation potentials. The key to the PPNM was
using the pollutant equivalent to replace the volume equivalent of
influent wastewater. Generally, when more pollutants are removed,
more GHGs are emitted (Delre et al., 2017), which could be directly
calculated by the parameters of pollutant in influent and effluent of
WWTPs. For CH4 calculation, the IPCC method uses per capita
biochemical oxygen demand (BOD5) and population to calculate the
organic matter in the wastewater. For N2O calculation, the IPCC 2006
method uses a population equivalent (3.2 g N2O per person/year) to
calculate GHG emissions, while the IPCC, 2006 method uses annual per
capita protein consumption and the protein nitrogen content (FNPR) to
represent the TN of the influent wastewater. Therefore, the BOD and TN
calculated using the IPCC method were determined by the population
severed of WWTPs, which were in fact, volume equivalents. In the
PPNM, pollutant removed was calculated by multiplying treatment
quantities and differences between pollutant concentrations of influent
and effluent, the GHG emissions were calculated by the pollutant
equivalent. Chemical oxygen demand (COD) and TN were selected as
the pollutant parameters, since they are the primary indicator of organic
matters and nitrogen removal efficiency in wastewater.
