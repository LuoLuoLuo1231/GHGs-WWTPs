4. Conclusions
Understanding and quantifying GHG emissions from wastewater
treatment is critically important for emission reduction and finally
achieving carbon neutrality. CH4 and N2O emissions from biological
processes have been demonstrated to contribute a large fraction in total
carbon footprint of WWTPs. This study presents a comprehensive survey
on CH4 and N2O emission monitoring through two measuring ap-
proaches at an underground municipal WWTP in southern China using
online instruments. The unit-based measurements using a flux chamber
focused on the emissions from individual process units of the WWTP
whereas the plant-integrated measurements quantified the emissions
through the off-gas collection systems of the whole plant. The main
conclusions of this study are:
• The results of unit-based measurements showed that aerobic zones
including the oxic tank and the aerated grit chamber are important
GHG emission sources. Around 10 times higher emissions were found
in plant-integrated measurements than unit-based measurements,
which was likely due to the omission of individual on-site emission
sources using the latter method and different survey timeframes of
the two approaches.
• The CH4 and N2O emission factors measured in this study fell within
the lower range reported in literatures, probably caused by different
influent quality and different operational conditions of the plants.
• Plant-integrated measurements serve as a feasible method when the
primary goal is to quantify overall GHG emissions of WWTPs.
However, the plant-integrated measurements in this study were
conducted as a short-term monitoring attempt to explore the feasi-
bility of the approach. A long-term study in the future will help
reducing the uncertainties of emission quantification.
• Furthermore, limitations in plant-specific local conditions may
hinder the application of plant-integrated measurements, prompting
the use of a bottom-up approach. This involves identifying and
quantifying the largest emitters at the unit-based level and then
aggregating these individual sources. Nevertheless, the unit-based
method may overlook unknown emission sources. In this context,
combining plant-integrated measurements with individual process
unit assessments can not only pinpoint the emission sources but also
enhance the accuracy of total GHG emission accounting of WWTPs.
