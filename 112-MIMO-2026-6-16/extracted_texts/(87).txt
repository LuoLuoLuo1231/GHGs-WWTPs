scenarioimpactstocreateintegratedwater–energy–GHGsolutions. Theanalysisoffour

highenergyself-sufficiency,andScenario5enabledsomeplantstoachievenet-zeroenergy
andcarbonconditions. Theapproachproposedinthisstudyprovidesactionableinsights

3. Methodology
This study performed a three-step procedure to develop customized strategies for
mitigatingGHGemissions(Figure3). First,unit-process-basedenergyconsumptionwas
quantitativelyestimatedbyanalyzingallunitprocesseswithineachWWTP.Second,direct
GHGemissionsfromtreatmentprocesses,effluentdischarge,andon-sitefuelburningas
wellasindirectemissionsfromelectricityusewerecalculated. Finally,feasiblescenarios
forGHGmitigationwereanalyzedtoidentifyeffectivemeasuresandtheirimpacts.
3.1. ComputationofUnit-Process-BasedEnergyConsumption
Measurementdatarequiredforestimatingenergyconsumedbytheunitprocesses
of the WWTPs were unavailable. Consequently, appropriate unit processes with their
intensities were selected from Reekie et al. [28] based on the plant-related information
provided by the SMG to describe the detailed layouts shown in Section 2. The unique
set of unit processes for individual WWTPs enables the characterization of all relevant
processes in a WWTP. Based on the energy intensity ranges of unit processes provided
byReekieetal.[28]andthedailyinfluentratesofWWTPs, theenergyconsumptionof
mostunitprocessesinindividualplantswasdetermined. Theenergyuseforodorcontrol
wasestimatedusingtheenergyintensityvaluesrecommendedbyCho[27]. Theenergy
intensityvaluesusedforodorcontrolwere0.0079,0.0075,0.0090,and0.0073kWh/m3for
theNanji,Jungrang,Tancheon,andSeonamplants,respectively. Thisunit-process-based
methodenablesstraightforwardapplicationtodifferentWWTPsbyincludingvariousunit
processes.Inaddition,thismethodisalsoappropriateforcomparingdifferentcombinations
oftreatmentprocessesortechnologies.
Thetotalenergyconsumptionwascalculatedbysummingtheenergyuseofindividual
unitprocesses. Subsequently,theenergyintensityofeachWWTPwasdeterminedbydivid-
ingthetotalenergyconsumptionbythetotalvolumeoftreatedwastewater. Inaddition,
thenetenergyconsumptionandnetenergyintensitywerealsoestimated,consideringthe
self-producedenergyfromtheWWTPs.

3.3. ScenarioDescription
Carbon neutrality in WWTPs can be achieved by reducing direct emissions from
wastewatertreatmentprocesses,reducingenergyconsumption(e.g.,improvingenergy
efficiency),andincreasingenergyharvestingfromwastewater(e.g.,energyrecovery)and
renewable energy use (e.g., solar energy) [49]. This study analyzed five future scenar-
ios for the year 2050 to evaluate the potential GHG mitigation of WWTPs. The effects
of these scenarios on energy consumption and GHG emissions were compared with a
baselinescenario.
ThepercapitaproductionofwastewaterinSMCincreasedfrom2015to2021(Figure1).
Thistrendwasassumedtocontinueuntil2050inthisstudy. Therefore,thetotalvolume
of treated wastewater for WWTPs in 2050 was estimated by multiplying the projected
populationservedbyWWTPsbypercapitawastewaterproduction.
