Tofu wastewater water treatment also faces challenges related to low strength and a low carbon-to-nitrogen (C/N) ratio. This
Greenhouse gas study investigates the high-carbon tofu wastewater flowing into municipal sewers for co-treatment at a waste-
Municipal wastewater
water treatment plant (WWTP) directly, with the goal of enhancing nitrogen removal and reduce GHG emissions.
Carbon emissions calculation
Within the framework of a circular economy for wastewater treatment, tofu wastewater serves as an external
Circular economy
carbon source for sustainable solutions. The concentrated tofu wastewater had an average chemical oxygen
demand (CODCr) of 21,894 ±11,485 mg/L, total nitrogen (TN) of 591.8 ±238.2 mg/L, and a C/N ratio of 36.9
±7.4. The denitrification rate reached 3.05 mg NO3(cid:0) -N/(g MLVSS⋅h). Therefore, tofu wastewater is a suitable
alternative carbon source. A full-scale WWTP with a capacity of 20,000 m3/day was monitored from 2017 to
2022 to evaluate the co-treatment effects of municipal wastewater and tofu wastewater. The results showed an
increase in 53.3% in the average CODCr concentration of the influent wastewater, while the total nitrogen and
total phosphorus removal efficiencies were enhanced to 75.8% and 95.2%, respectively. In addition, the study
quantified GHG emissions from tofu wastewater and municipal wastewater treatment. Compared to separate
treatment processes, the co-treatment reduced GHG emissions by 337.9 t CO2-eq., approximately 15.8% of the
total emissions of WWTP, and achieved a cost saving of 7–10% of the total operational costs. These findings
demonstrate the environmental and economic advantages of integrating high-carbon industrial wastewater
treatment directly into wastewater treatment plants.
